Behavioral Lab

Tools to investigate what happened, reconstruct what should have happened, and create objective interruptions before a bias or emotion becomes a chain of decisions.

Scope: these tools organize self-report. They do not diagnose disorders, determine whether a trade is financially correct, or replace risk rules. Data stays in this browser.
01 · PAST

Session behavioral autopsy

Reconstruct the episode in chronological order. Look for the first deviation, not only the largest loss.

02 · CHAIN

Trigger → self-talk → emotion → action

03 · TILT

Escalation checklist

Rate the observed intensity. The result reflects self-reported behavioral load, not a diagnosis.

Complete all items.

04 · CORRECTIVE REPLAY

Rehearse the correct response

Use replay or simulation. Do not change the correct response using information that only became available later.

Repeat the scene until you can name the trigger and execute the planned response without using the future outcome as a clue. There is no magic number of repetitions.

05 · ASYMMETRY

Educational detector: short winners, long losers

Enter statistics from a sample of your history. This flags asymmetries; it does not prove the disposition effect or account for your strategy's logic.

Enter all four values.

06 · CONTEXT RESET

Periodic context reading

The default interval is 15 minutes, but it is configurable. It has no specific scientific validation; adapt it to your timeframe and method.

07 · HERE AND NOW

A.G.O.R.A. radar

Use while reviewing a losing position or during replay. Notice when attention shifts from the current market to the past, an imagined reversal, or hypothetical profit.

A reversal can be part of an analysis; the error is turning it into a justification for breaking the current rule.
