Behavioral practice

A 14-session learning path

Observe triggers, reframe loss, precommit to responses, and consolidate process metrics. Fourteen sessions do not mean fourteen trading days; exercises can be done away from a live market using replay or simulation.

Rule: no exercise asks you to create real losses to “practice the stop.” Prefer historical review, replay, or simulation. Real trades belong only to your normal plan.

Four phases

PHASE 1

Observe

Separate facts, thoughts, emotions, and urges.

PHASE 2

Reframe

Practice probabilistic language and reduce identity-based judgment.

PHASE 3

Precommit

Decide responses before pressure rises.

PHASE 4

Consolidate

Evaluate execution by process and review patterns.

The 14 sessions

SESSION 1

My story about stops

Write three phrases your mind associates with a stop, such as “failure,” “giving up,” or “it will come back.” Record them without correcting them.

SESSION 2

Fact versus narrative

Review three past stops. For each, write one line of observable fact and one of emotional interpretation.

SESSION 3

Body and impulse map

Identify early signals of interference: tension, urgency, fixation on P&L, reaching for the mouse, increasing size, or moving an order.

SESSION 4

A professional definition

Complete: “A stop is ______ when ______ because ______.” Include hypothesis, limit, and process.

SESSION 5

Automatic thought

Use the CBT thought record in Tools for a recurring thought.

SESSION 6

Cognitive defusion

Turn “it will come back” into “I am noticing the thought that it will come back.” Notice the difference without trying to expel the phrase.

SESSION 7

Outcome is not process

Choose a well-executed loss and a poorly executed win. Score the process without using P&L as the criterion.

SESSION 8

If–then plan

Create responses for critical triggers: widening the stop, revenge trading, and FOMO re-entry.

SESSION 9

Stop card

Reduce your stop protocol to four lines that can be read quickly under pressure.

SESSION 10

Replay rehearsal

Practice executing invalidation without renegotiation. The goal is behavior, not simulated profitability.

SESSION 11

Counterfactual regret

Review a case where price reversed after the stop. Ask “what did I know before?” rather than “what became obvious afterward?”

SESSION 12

Uncertainty

List what was controllable and uncontrollable in three trades. A stop does not resolve uncertainty; it limits the consequence.

SESSION 13

Disciplined self-compassion

Write what you would say to a competent colleague who made the same mistake, then add one concrete corrective action.

SESSION 14

Pattern review

Compare your sessions. Which triggers recur? Which responses work? Choose one behavioral rule to practice next.

The path can be repeated. The goal is not to eliminate emotions in fourteen steps, but to build a repertoire and data about your own behavior.
