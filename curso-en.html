Practical course · 8 modules

Learn to Execute a Stop

A course for turning a stop into an operational skill: define invalidation before entry, size risk, execute without emotional renegotiation, respond appropriately to a loss, and rehearse moments when the process failed.

Important: this course is educational. It does not prescribe assets, position sizes, risk percentages, or stop levels for real trades. Exercises use fictional scenarios and should be adapted to the student's method.

MODULE 1

What does it mean to execute a stop correctly?

Not completed

Executing a stop correctly does not mean exiting at the best price. It means following an invalidation and risk rule defined before the trade outcome puts pressure on the decision.

Core rule: the purpose of a stop is not to predict the next move. It is to end exposure when the previously defined condition for staying in the trade is no longer valid.

MODULE 2

Define the stop before entry

Not completed

The technical stop should come from the logic of the trade. First identify the point or condition at which the hypothesis no longer justifies staying in; then adjust position size to the acceptable risk. Doing the opposite—choosing the position size and then tightening the stop to make it “fit”—mixes technical analysis with financial need.

MODULE 4

Short wins, long losses

Not completed

The disposition effect describes a studied tendency: realizing gains early and holding on to losses. The purpose of this module is not to force traders to “let profits run” in every strategy, but to prevent being positive and being negative from replacing the method's criteria.

MODULE 5

After the stop: do not turn a loss into a mission

Not completed

A realized loss belongs to the past. The next trade has no obligation to compensate for it. The central risk is changing the purpose of the next trade: from taking a valid opportunity to regulating anger, shame, or the urgency to get back to zero.

Not completed
