Practical course · 8 modules

Learn to Execute a Stop

Practice defining invalidation before entry, sizing risk, executing without emotional renegotiation, responding to losses, and rehearsing moments when the process failed.

Important: educational content only. It does not prescribe instruments, position sizes, risk percentages, or stop levels for real trades. Use fictional scenarios and adapt them to your own method.

Execute without renegotiating the loss

Near the stop, the mind may invent reasons to delay. Distinguish a rule-based adjustment planned in advance from an adjustment made to avoid realizing a loss.

MODULE 4

Short winners, long losers

The disposition effect describes a tendency to realize gains early and hold losses too long. The aim is not to force profits to run in every strategy, but to stop P&L from replacing the method's criteria.

MODULE 5

After the stop: no recovery mission

The next trade has no debt to the previous one. A new entry must stand on its own merits and fit the plan; it is not a tool for returning to breakeven.
