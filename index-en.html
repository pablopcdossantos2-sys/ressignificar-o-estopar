Psychology applied to trading

A stop-loss is not giving up. It is executing a risk decision.

A learning resource for reframing the stop from a symbol of defeat into a professional skill: recognizing biases, tolerating uncertainty and discomfort, following predefined rules, and evaluating decisions by process rather than by an isolated outcome.

Why stops hurt

Loss aversion, the disposition effect, shame, regret, sunk costs, and the need to be right.

Separate loss from failure

CBT, ACT, self-compassion, and process-focused language help remove personal judgment from the stop.

03 · PRACTICE

Build reliable responses

Precommitment, if–then plans, replay, process metrics, and post-stop protocols reduce improvisation under pressure.

“A stop does not have to prove that you were wrong as a person. It closes a hypothesis under the risk conditions you chose to accept before the trade.”

The goal is not to make losses pleasant. It is to prevent discomfort from rewriting the plan.
